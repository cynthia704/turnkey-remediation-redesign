Where Is It Showing Up?

Mold follows moisture. Pick the part of your building closest to what you're seeing.

Call (504) 285-5666

Stained Ceiling Tiles

Why here

Roof and pipe leaks soak the tile from above, and the tile holds on to the water. See commercial water damage.

Look for

Brown rings, dark patches or a musty smell at your desk.

Tell us when you call

Which floor, how many tiles and whether it's still dripping.

Call (504) 285-5666

Restroom Walls and Ceilings

Why here

Slow leaks and poor airflow leave moisture with nowhere to go.

Look for

Dark patches around vents and behind fixtures.

Tell us when you call

Whether the fan works and if a fixture has leaked.

Call (504) 285-5666

Kitchens and Break Rooms

Why here

Sinks and cooking add moisture, and a pipe defect can keep feeding the problem.

Look for

Discoloration under sinks and behind appliances.

Tell us when you call

Whether food service is affected and what hours you run.

Call (504) 285-5666

Storage and Back Rooms

Why here

Closed doors and little airflow keep damp air in, and boxes against a wall hide what's behind them.

Look for

A musty smell when the door opens, and spots low on the wall.

Tell us when you call

What's stored there and whether it can be moved.

Call (504) 285-5666

Outside Walls

Why here

Defects in a wall let water in, and New Orleans humidity does the rest.

Look for

Patches around windows and low on the wall, from black to green or white.

Tell us when you call

Which side of the building, and whether there was a recent storm.

Commercial Mold Questions

Should My Staff Clean It?

We suggest leaving it alone until we've seen it. Scrubbing can spread spores, which is why we contain the area before removing anything.

Can We Stay Open During the Work?

It depends on where it is and how much of the building needs closing off. We'll talk it through with you before any work starts.

Does Insurance Cover It?

That depends on your policy and what caused the moisture. Your agent can tell you what applies.

What If Water Is Still Coming In?

The source has to stop first, because mold needs moisture to keep growing. See commercial water damage.
