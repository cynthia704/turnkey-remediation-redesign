After a Kitchen Fire in Harahan

A stovetop flare-up at home or a grease fire on a restaurant line can leave three separate messes. Choose the one in front of you to see what helps and what makes it worse.

Soot Smears Easily

  • Skip the wet rag. Soot smears into paint and grout when you wipe it damp.
  • Keep foot traffic off light carpet so the black film doesn't grind in.
  • Throw a sheet over furniture, or over booths and tables in a dining room, that stayed clean until the work starts.
  • Our fire damage restoration page shows how a room gets cleaned from top to bottom.

The Smell Hides in Fabric

  • Curtains, couch cushions and closet clothes soak up smoke and hold on to it.
  • Candles and sprays cover the odor for an hour, then it comes right back.
  • Swap the AC filter so the system stops pushing smoky air through the house or the office.
  • For a smell that won't quit, see odor removal.

Wet Floors After the Fire Crew

  • Fire hoses put a lot of water into a kitchen in just a few minutes.
  • Lift rugs and pick up wet things from the floor, as long as you can do it without risk.
  • Stay out of any room where the ceiling sags or drips.
  • We dry hose water the same way we dry a burst pipe. See water damage restoration.

All About Harahan

Harahan, a Railroad Town

Harahan is a small Jefferson Parish city along the Mississippi River, just upriver from New Orleans. It carries the name of James Theodore Harahan, who ran the Illinois Central Railroad.

  • ParishJefferson
  • Population9,116 at the 2020 census
  • Incorporated1920, as a village
  • ZIP Code70123

Things to Do in Harahan

  1. 01

    The Levee Path

    A paved trail on top of the Mississippi River levee, popular for walks, runs and bike rides.

  2. 02

    Park of Heroes

    A memorial park dedicated in 2025 to Harahan residents who died in military service.

  3. 03

    The Pecan Festival

    A parish fair at St. Rita on Jefferson Highway, with live bands, rides, games and food.

Landmarks Harahan Elementary School, Zeringue Park, the Mississippi River levee, the Huey P. Long Bridge

Harahan Elementary School

The school on Jefferson Highway opened to students in 1926, in a two-story brick building designed by William R. Burk.

It was listed on the National Register in 1983.

Fun Facts About Harahan

  1. 1James Theodore Harahan, the city's namesake, led the Illinois Central Railroad from 1906 to 1911.
  2. 2The land was a demonstration farm until 1914, when a group of Illinois Central stockholders bought it and started the town.
  3. 3Railroads ferried their cars across the Mississippi between Harahan and Avondale until December 1935.
  4. 4The ferry ended when the Huey P. Long Bridge opened. It was the first bridge across the Mississippi River in Louisiana.
