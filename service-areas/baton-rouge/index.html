Look After the Household First

  • Cut power to any room where water has reached outlets or cords, if the panel sits somewhere dry.
  • Carry photos, papers and rugs to a dry room or a second floor.
  • Take pictures of each wet room before anything goes to the curb, then call your insurance agent.
  • Our residential water damage restoration page explains how a house gets dried out, and carpet cleaning can help with rugs caught early.

Keep People Working Where You Can

  • Unplug computers and pull paper records off wet carpet tiles.
  • Lift merchandise off the bottom shelves closest to the leak.
  • Tape off the wet area or put out signs so nobody slips.
  • Notify building management or your landlord, and write down when you did.
  • For routine upkeep, look at ceiling cleaning and wall cleaning.

Protect the Line and the Loading Dock

  • After any kitchen fire, throw out open food that smoke reached.
  • Grease and soot cling to hoods and vents, so our odor removal and air duct cleaning pages cover what a quick wipe leaves behind.
  • In a warehouse, shift pallets to dry racks and photograph damaged goods with their labels showing.
  • Notice any musty smell on walls or cardboard in the weeks after, and look at commercial mold removal if it shows up.
  • Big concrete floors may need machine scrubbing through our floor cleaning service.

All About Baton Rouge

Baton Rouge, the State Capital

Baton Rouge is the capital of Louisiana and the seat of East Baton Rouge Parish, set along the Mississippi. Its name is French for red stick.

  • ParishEast Baton Rouge
  • Population227,470 at the 2020 census
  • Incorporated1817
  • ZIP Codes70801, 70802, 70806, 70808 and more

Things to Do in Baton Rouge

  1. 01

    Louisiana State Capitol

    At 450 feet and 34 floors, it's the tallest state capitol building in the country.

  2. 02

    Old State Capitol

    A Gothic, castle-style statehouse built from 1847 to 1852, now a museum of Louisiana political history.

  3. 03

    USS Kidd Veterans Museum

    A World War II destroyer moored on the Mississippi downtown, with a museum building beside it.

Landmarks the Louisiana State Capitol, the Old State Capitol, LSU's Tiger Stadium, the Horace Wilkinson Bridge

Baton Rouge Fun Facts

  1. 1A French expedition in 1699 spotted a red pole marking a hunting boundary, and the place became known as the red stick.
  2. 2LSU moved to Baton Rouge in 1869 after its building in Pineville burned.
  3. 3The Horace Wilkinson Bridge opened in 1968 and carries I-10 over the Mississippi. Locals call it the New Bridge.
  4. 4The Port of Greater Baton Rouge is the farthest-upstream port on the Mississippi that can handle Panamax ships.
