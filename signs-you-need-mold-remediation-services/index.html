Quick Answer

A musty smell that keeps coming back, dark spots on a wall or ceiling, and paint that bubbles after a leak are the usual signs that mold may be growing. TurnKey Remediation handles mold removal by containing the area, removing affected materials and cleaning what can be saved. This guide covers what you see, what is probably behind it and what to do in the next day.

Why Mold Shows Up Where It Does

Mold needs moisture. The EPA says none of the many types of mold will grow without water or moisture, which is why the signs below almost always trace back to a wet spot.

Whether it is a house that needs residential mold removal or a shop, office or restaurant that needs commercial mold removal, the starting point is the same. Find where the water came from.

Timing matters too. The CDC advises that when water gets into a building, wet items should be cleaned and dried or removed within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold from growing.

Mold also hides. FEMA notes it may be hidden under or behind items like flooring, appliances or walls.

Signs You Can See or Smell

The CDC says mold can look like spots of many different colors, and that it can smell musty. These are the signs most worth noticing, in a house or a business.

A Musty Smell That Keeps Coming Back

The EPA says you may suspect hidden mold if a building smells moldy but you cannot see the source. Air fresheners only cover the smell.

Dark or Discolored Spots on Walls or Ceilings

Spots can be dark, green or fuzzy and white, like the ones in the photo above. Any spot like that is a reason to look for moisture nearby.

Peeling or Bubbling Paint or Wallpaper

Paint lifting off a wall or wallpaper curling at the seams often means water is behind it. FEMA is plain that painting or caulking over mold will not stop it from growing.

Water Stains or Warped Materials

A brown ring on a ceiling tile, a swollen baseboard or a buckling floor all tell you water has been there. The surface may look dry while the material behind it is not.

One Sign Is Worth Watching

One sign does not mean mold, but moisture may be in the wrong place. Look for the water source, dry the area and watch for new spots or a smell that returns.

What Is Probably Behind Each Sign

Almost every sign has a water source behind it, and finding it decides whether mold comes back.

Leaks, Floods and Roof Damage

The CDC says mold will grow where there is moisture, such as around leaks in roofs, windows or pipes, or where there has been a flood. When the water was a bigger event, our water damage restoration work covers drying out and repairing, for a house or for a business.

Humid Air and Condensation

The EPA says condensation can be a sign of high humidity. Water beading on a window or a cold pipe is worth noticing, and so is air that feels sticky.

The Smell Without a Source

When a smell lingers and nothing is visible, the cause may be behind a wall. Our odor removal and air duct and HVAC cleaning pages cover the cleanup once the moisture is dealt with.

What to Do in the Next Day

Start with the water, then leave the area as it is.

Find and Stop the Moisture Source

The EPA says to fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible, and that if you clean up mold but leave the water problem, the mold will most likely come back.

Dry Wet Items Completely

The EPA advises drying items completely, and FEMA says items that cannot be cleaned and dried within 24 to 48 hours must be removed and thrown away.

Keep the Area Closed Off

FEMA advises people to stay away from moldy sites. Close the door and keep people out of that room.

Call While You Wait

If you have found it, or you are not sure what you are looking at, call us. We answer 24/7.

What Not to Do

The urge to scrub it off right away is strong. Hold off.

  • Do not paint or caulk over it. FEMA says it will not stop the growth, and the EPA says not to paint or caulk moldy surfaces.
  • Do not scrub or brush a spot you are unsure about. Leave it alone and call.
  • Do not cover a smell with air fresheners. The source is still there.
  • Do not ignore a leak because the stain looks dry.

The EPA publishes its own cleanup guidance for small moldy areas. If the growth is larger, or you are not sure, leave the area alone and call.

Homes and Businesses Show the Same Signs

A restaurant with a drip behind the dish station and a house with a roof leak over a bedroom closet can show the same warning signs. The first move is the same too: stop the water, close off the area and call.

Common Questions

What Does Mold Smell Like?

The CDC says mold can smell musty. A smell like that which keeps coming back in the same room is worth following to its source.

Can I Just Paint Over Mold?

No. FEMA says painting or caulking over mold will not prevent it from growing, and the EPA says to clean and dry a surface rather than coat it. The water problem behind it also needs to be fixed.

How Fast Can Mold Grow After Water Damage?

The EPA, CDC and FEMA all point to a drying window of 24 to 48 hours. The point of the window is to dry things before mold has a chance to start.

Can Mold Be Hidden Behind Walls?

Yes. FEMA and the EPA both say it may be hidden behind walls, under flooring or behind wallpaper. A smell with no visible source is one clue.

What Should I Do If I Am Not Sure It Is Mold?

Keep the area closed off, leave it alone and stop any moisture source you can find. Then call (504) 285-5666. We answer 24/7.
